# ImageGen Concept Analysis — Zombie Liberation V2

Generated source saved in project:

- `assets/concept/zombie-liberation-art-bible-v2.png`

## Verdict

Accepted as the new visual direction reference.

The image works because:

- It clearly shows the core fantasy: 5 heroes, 5 map regions, silly zombie enemies, and one final boss.
- The mood is bright, hopeful, and child-safe rather than scary.
- The silhouettes are readable enough to inspire small game sprites.
- The top-right / path-map idea is visually clear and matches the implemented UI map.
- The palette fits a 10-year-old creator's browser game: saturated blues, greens, yellows, pinks, and purple boss-zone contrast.

## Things to keep from the concept

- Wide adventure-map composition.
- Five compact hero silhouettes:
  - lightning hero,
  - shield defender,
  - rocket-energy attacker,
  - spark tactician,
  - lion finisher.
- Goofy big zombie boss: round, expressive, not frightening.
- Small silly zombie minions: awkward and funny, no horror.
- Country regions represented by environment cues rather than exact flags.
- Bright mobile-game readability.

## Things to correct before sprite generation

The concept is good for direction, but final sprites must tighten these rules:

- Do not use exact real-world monuments as required sprite assets.
- Do not use exact national flags or official emblems.
- Do not use readable text, logos, or brand marks.
- Do not use realistic weapons. The rocket fighter should use a toy-like energy launcher or rocket backpack cue, not a firearm-like object.
- Do not add detached effects, heavy glows, shadows, smoke, speed lines, blood, wounds, or gore.

## Final production decision

Use this concept as the art bible for palette, proportions, and emotional tone. Generate characters and NPCs through a Hatch Pet-like sprite workflow with locked identity, compact full-body silhouettes, chroma-key/transparent-ready backgrounds, and per-state animation strips.
